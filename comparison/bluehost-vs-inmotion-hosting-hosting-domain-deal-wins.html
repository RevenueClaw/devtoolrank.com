Bluehost vs InMotion Hosting: Which Hosting or Domain Deal Wins in 2026?

Bluehost and InMotion Hosting get compared a lot, but usually for the wrong reason. People type "Bluehost vs Inmotion hosting" expecting a straight price fight, then discover that the two companies barely compete for the same customer.

Bluehost is the brand you've heard on every second YouTube tutorial: cheap intro pricing, a WordPress onboarding flow that holds your hand, and a free domain thrown in. InMotion Hosting is the one your more experienced friend mentions quietly — US-based support that answers in minutes, data centers they actually own, and a 90-day refund window instead of the usual 30.

So which one deserves your money in 2026? I've run sites on both, migrated between them, and argued with both support teams. Here's the honest breakdown, including where each one falls short.

The 30-second verdict

  • Pick InMotion Hosting if you care about speed, ticket response times, and not being upsold six times during checkout.
  • Pick Bluehost if you want the cheapest possible first-year entry point, a free domain, and a WordPress setup that walks you through every step.

Pricing: the intro price is not the price

Both companies advertise monthly rates that require a 12-, 24-, or 36-month prepayment. Both raise the price at renewal. This is industry standard and not a scam, but the gap between the two at renewal is bigger than the gap at signup, and that's the number that actually matters in year two.

Two things jump out.

First, Bluehost's Basic plan is aggressively cheap — under three bucks a month on a three-year term is genuinely hard to beat, and it's why so many first sites start there. Second, look at the storage. InMotion's cheapest tier gives you 100 GB. Bluehost's cheapest gives you 10 GB. If you're hosting a photography portfolio or anything with a media library, that difference shows up fast.

The renewal math is where Bluehost's advantage evaporates. Basic jumping from $2.95 to roughly $10.99 is a 3.7x increase. InMotion's Launch doubles from $4.99 to about $9.99. On a three-year horizon, InMotion's entry plan often ends up cheaper overall despite the higher sticker price on day one.

Also worth noting: InMotion's monthly billing is actually available and not absurd, while Bluehost's cheapest rates are locked behind long prepay terms. If you hate committing three years to a host you haven't tested, that flexibility matters.

Performance and infrastructure

This is InMotion's strongest card, and it's not close.

InMotion runs its own data centers in Ashburn, Virginia and Los Angeles, California. They're privately held, US-based, and they own the hardware their shared plans sit on. That control shows up in consistent time-to-first-byte numbers — usually in the 150–400 ms range on a cached WordPress page from a US test location, depending on plan and traffic.

Bluehost sits under Newfold Digital (the company formerly known as EIG), which owns a long list of hosting brands sharing infrastructure. Performance is fine, but it's more variable. You might land on a server that's snappy, or you might land on a neighbor-heavy box that sags under load. Bluehost has improved this over the past few years with better resource isolation on the Pro tier, but the baseline experience across plans is less predictable.

Practical translation: if you're building a site where Core Web Vitals matter — a store, a SaaS marketing site, anything with SEO pressure — InMotion gives you a better starting point. If you're launching a blog that gets 500 visits a month, either one is more than enough and you should optimize your images instead of worrying about the host.

Neither company is the fastest host on the market. If raw speed is your only metric, you're looking at Cloudways, SiteGround, or a VPS. But between these two specifically, InMotion wins on consistency.
