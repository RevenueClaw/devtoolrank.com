ExpressVPN vs OrionVPN: Which VPN Is Worth Your Money in 2026?

Comparing ExpressVPN to OrionVPN is a bit like comparing a name-brand cordless drill to a generic one from a marketplace listing. One has fifteen years of independent audits, a public bug bounty, and a price tag that makes people wince. The other is cheap, small, and genuinely hard to verify from the outside.

That doesn't automatically Make the cheap one a bad buy. But it does change what you're actually paying for, and it's the part most comparison articles skip.

This breakdown covers both providers on the things that matter when you're actually using a VPN: speed, logging policy, streaming, device limits, support, and — the question nobody likes — whether the company will still exist when your subscription renews. There's a full feature table, a pricing table, honest pros and cons for each, and a verdict at the end that picks one.

The 30-second answer

ExpressVPN wins for most people. It's faster, better audited, works with more streaming services, and has 24/7 live chat you can reach at 2am when your connection breaks. You pay roughly three to four times more for that.

OrionVPN wins on one axis only: price. If your budget is genuinely $30–50 per year and nothing else fits, it's a functional VPN. Just go in knowing that "functional" and "trustworthy" aren't the same word.

Now the details.

A note on sourcing, because it matters here

ExpressVPN publishes audit reports, a transparency report, a warrant canary, and detailed technical documentation about its server architecture. OrionVPN publishes a marketing site, an app, and not much else.

That asymmetry is itself a finding. When we couldn't independently verify a claim about OrionVPN, we say so below rather than repeating the marketing copy as fact. Where pricing is involved, we note that smaller providers change prices often — verify before you enter card details. Everything about ExpressVPN here reflects its current published documentation and our own testing.

Company background and jurisdiction

ExpressVPN launched in 2009 and is headquartered in the British Virgin Islands, which has no mandatory data retention laws and no intelligence-sharing agreements with the US, UK, or EU. In 2021, Kape Technologies acquired it for $936 million.
