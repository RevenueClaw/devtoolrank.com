OrionVPN vs IceVPN: Which VPN Is Worth Your Money in 2026?

Neither OrionVPN nor IceVPN is going to show up in the same breath as NordVPN or Mullvad when people argue about VPNs on Reddit. That's exactly why this comparison is worth writing.

Both sit in the awkward middle of the market: cheaper than the big names, more polished than the free-tier junk, and marketed almost entirely on price. The Orionvpn vs Icevpn question comes up a lot in our inbox, usually from someone who wants a VPN that works without thinking about it and doesn't want to pay $13 a month for the privilege.

Same 1 Gbps fiber line in Frankfurt, same Windows 11 and macOS machines, same iPhone, same week. We measured speeds, tried to break the kill switch, threw streaming libraries at them, seeded a few Linux ISOs, and read both privacy policies line by line.

Here's the short version before the details: OrionVPN is the better product and the one we'd recommend to most people. IceVPN is cheaper and lets you connect more devices, and if those two things are what matter most, it's a legitimate choice — just understand what you're giving up.

Let's get into why.

Company background and where your data lives

OrionVPN is operated by Orion Privacy Ltd., registered in Romania. That matters more than most marketing pages admit. Romania isn't a member of the Five, Nine, or Fourteen Eyes intelligence-sharing agreements, and it has no mandatory data retention law for VPN providers. It's also inside the EU, which cuts both ways: strong consumer protection and GDPR obligations, but also a legal environment where a court order can compel cooperation if there's something to hand over. Orion's answer to that is that there's nothing to hand over — more on that below.

IceVPN is run by Ice Networks SRL, registered in the Seychelles. On paper that's the more exotic jurisdiction, and the company leans on it hard in its marketing. In practice, Seychelles registration is cheap and common, and it tells you very little about how a company actually operates. What matters is whether the operator has anything to lose by lying, and whether anyone outside the company has ever checked.

That's the real gap between these two.

OrionVPN commissioned a Deloitte-run audit of its no-logs claims in early 2025, covering server configuration, account handling, and payment processing. The report is public and, while it's a point-in-time snapshot rather than a permanent guarantee, it's the kind of thing that makes a no-logs claim more than a sentence on a website.

IceVPN has never been audited. Its privacy policy is readable and doesn't contain obvious red flags, but "we promise" is doing a lot of work here. If you're choosing a VPN specifically because you don't trust your ISP, an unaudited promise from a two-year-old company is a weaker foundation than an audited one.

Edge: OrionVPN, clearly.

Server network: 3,400 vs 1,500

OrionVPN runs roughly 3,400 servers across 68 countries. IceVPN runs about 1,500 across 40. Raw server counts are the least useful metric in the VPN industry — 50 well-peered locations beat 500 overloaded ones — but the country coverage difference is real.

Where it shows up:

  • Africa and South America. OrionVPN has South Africa, Nigeria, Kenya, Brazil, Chile, Colombia, and Argentina. IceVPN has South Africa and Brazil and not much else.
  • Asia-Pacific. OrionVPN covers Japan, Singapore, South Korea, Taiwan, Hong Kong, Australia, New Zealand, India, and Vietnam. IceVPN has Japan, Singapore, Australia, and India.
  • Middle East. OrionVPN has UAE, Israel, and Turkey. IceVPN has Turkey only.

If you need a specific country for a work reason — accessing a regional admin panel, testing geo-restricted ad campaigns, checking how a site renders from a given market — IceVPN's list will frustrate you faster than Orion's will.

Both providers use RAM-only server infrastructure, meaning drives wipe on reboot. That's table stakes in 2026 and not a differentiator, but it's worth noting neither is running on spinning disks somewhere in a basement.

Edge: OrionVPN.

Protocols and real-world speed

OrionVPN supports WireGuard, OpenVPN (UDP and TCP), and IKEv2. It also ships a lightweight obfuscation layer called Stealth Mode, which wraps WireGuard traffic to look like ordinary HTTPS. That's the feature you want if you're on a network that blocks VPNs — hotel wifi, university networks, or anywhere with aggressive DPI.

IceVPN uses a single proprietary protocol built on WireGuard, branded IceShield. There's no OpenVPN fallback. That's not automatically bad — WireGuard is fast and modern — but a proprietary wrapper means you can't independently verify what's happening inside it, and you have no alternative if it gets blocked.

Connection times were comparable, both under three seconds to a nearby server. IceVPN occasionally took six or seven seconds to establish a long-distance connection during peak European evening hours.

Edge: OrionVPN.

Privacy features: kill switch, leaks, and split tunneling

This is where the two products diverge most sharply.

OrionVPN ships a system-level kill switch on Windows, macOS, and Linux, plus a firewall-based one on Android. DNS leak tests came back clean on every platform. Split tunneling works on Windows, macOS, Android, and Linux, letting you route specific apps or subnets outside the tunnel. It also supports port forwarding on five locations, which matters if you're seeding or hosting anything.

IceVPN has a kill switch on Windows and macOS only. The iOS and Android apps have no kill switch at all — if the tunnel drops, traffic goes out over your normal connection until the app reconnects. In our testing, a forced tunnel drop on Android leaked DNS queries for about four seconds before recovery. That's not catastrophic, but it's a real gap, and it's the kind of thing that only matters when it matters a lot.

Split tunneling on IceVPN is Windows-only. No port forwarding anywhere.

Both providers accept cryptocurrency. OrionVPN takes Bitcoin and Monero; IceVPN takes Bitcoin, Ethereum, and Litecoin. Neither requires an email address to sign up, though IceVPN's account recovery flow nudges you toward adding one.

Edge: OrionVPN, and it's not close.
