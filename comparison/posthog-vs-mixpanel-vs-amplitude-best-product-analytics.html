PostHog vs Mixpanel vs Amplitude: Best Product Analytics Platform

There's a specific moment in a product's life when you realize you have no idea what your users are actually doing. You shipped the onboarding flow, the signups went up, and then... nothing. You're staring at a dashboard that tells you traffic exists but not why people leave.

That's when you start shopping for product analytics, and you hit the same three names every time: PostHog, Mixpanel, and Amplitude. They look similar on the surface — event tracking, funnels, retention charts — but they're built for genuinely different kinds of teams, and picking wrong costs you either money or months of migration pain.

I've wired all three into real projects. Here's what actually matters.

The short answer

PostHog is the best pick for solo developers, indie hackers, and small teams who want analytics plus session replay, feature flags, and experiments from one vendor without per-seat pricing. Mixpanel is the best pure analytics tool if you want deep funnel and retention analysis and don't need the rest of the suite. Amplitude is the enterprise choice — more expensive, more governance, better for large orgs with data teams.

If you want the one-liner: start with PostHog unless you have a specific reason not to. Now let's get into the reasons.

Three different philosophies

The pricing pages don't tell you this, but these products were built with different assumptions about who you are.

PostHog is the "all-in-one" bet. Founded in 2020, it started as an open-source alternative to Mixpanel and kept bolting on products: session replay, feature flags, A/B testing, surveys, a data warehouse, error tracking, and LLM observability. The pitch is that you replace four or five vendors with one SDK and one bill.

Mixpanel is the specialist. It's been around since 2009, and in 2020 it deliberately killed its messaging and engagement products to focus entirely on analytics. That focus shows in the query engine and the depth of the funnel and retention tooling. It's a company that decided to do one thing well rather than five things adequately.

Amplitude is the platform play for bigger orgs. It IPO'd in 2021, sells primarily to enterprise, and its product set (Analytics, Experiment, Audience, Session Replay, Guides and Surveys) is designed to plug into a data stack that already includes Snowflake or BigQuery. It's the tool you buy when you have a data team and a procurement process.

Feature breakdown

Event tracking and data model

All three use the standard event-property model: you send events with properties, identify users, and group them into cohorts. The differences are in the details.

PostHog's SDKs are the most developer-friendly of the three. Autocapture is on by default, which means you get click and pageview data before you've written a single tracking plan. That's a huge win for small teams — you can instrument properly later. It also supports group analytics for B2B (companies, workspaces) on all plans, which Mixpanel and Amplitude gate behind higher tiers in some configurations.

Mixpanel requires more deliberate instrumentation. You define events and properties, and the quality of your output depends heavily on the quality of that plan. In exchange you get a very fast query engine and a data model that rewards good naming conventions.

Amplitude sits in between. Its taxonomy tools are the strongest of the three for enforcing naming conventions across a large org, which matters when you have 40 engineers shipping events.
