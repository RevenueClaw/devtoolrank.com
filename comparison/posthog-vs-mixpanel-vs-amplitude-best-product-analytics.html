PostHog vs Mixpanel vs Amplitude: Which Product Analytics Platform Actually Fits Your Stack?

Analytics belongs in every indie stack — our 10 best developer tools for indie hackers covers the tools we’d actually pay for.

You shipped something. A few hundred people signed up. Now you want to know why 80% of them never came back on day two, and whether the ones who did clicked the pricing page first or the docs first.

That's the moment you go looking for a product analytics tool, and you land on the same three names everyone else lands on: PostHog, Mixpanel, and Amplitude. They all do "product analytics." They all have a free tier. They all claim to be the obvious choice.

They are not interchangeable. Here's the short version before we get into the details:

  • PostHog is the pick if you want analytics, session replay, and feature flags in one place — and you care about being able to self-host or export your way out of a vendor lock-in problem later.
  • Mixpanel is the pick if funnel and retention analysis is your primary job and you want the most generous free tier in the category.
  • Amplitude is the pick if you're past the indie stage and need governance, behavioral cohorts, and a tool your data team won't complain about.

Everything below is the reasoning behind those three sentences.

What each tool actually is

PostHog started in 2020 out of Y Combinator as an open-source alternative to Mixpanel and Amplitude. The core is MIT-licensed, it runs on ClickHouse, and you can deploy it yourself with Docker Compose or Helm. It began as pure event analytics and grew sideways into a suite: session replay, feature flags, A/B testing, surveys, error tracking, a data warehouse, and LLM analytics.

Mixpanel is the oldest of the three, founded in 2009. It's a specialist — event-based analytics with a heavy focus on funnels, retention curves, and cohort breakdowns. It added session replay in 2024, but analytics is still the center of gravity. Pricing moved to a monthly-tracked-users model, which is friendlier than the old event-volume tiers if you have a small number of very active users.

Amplitude launched in 2012 and went upmarket early. It's the one you'll find at companies with a dedicated data or growth team. Amplitude's differentiators are behavioral cohorts (build a cohort from any chart, then sync it to your email or ads tool), a data catalog for taxonomy enforcement, and an experimentation product. It acquired Command AI in 2024 to push into in-app guidance and AI agents.

Feature breakdown

Data model and tracking

All three are event-based: you send event_name plus properties, and they attach it to a user.

PostHog uses events + person profiles + groups. Groups are how you track company-level or workspace-level activity without stuffing everything into user properties. Autocapture means you can get baseline click and pageview tracking on web without writing a single track() call, which is genuinely useful in the first week when you don't know what you want to measure yet.

Mixpanel uses events + user profiles + group analytics. Group analytics used to be a paid add-on and is now part of the higher tiers. Mixpanel's tracking is more explicit — you define events and properties, and there's less magic.

Amplitude uses events + user properties + group properties, and adds a Data Catalog that lets you define a taxonomy centrally and flag events that don't conform. If you've ever inherited an analytics account with signup, Sign_Up, signed up, and user_created all meaning the same thing, you understand why that matters.

Funnels, retention, and cohorts

This is where Mixpanel and Amplitude still earn their keep.

Mixpanel's funnel tool is the most pleasant of the three to use. You get step-by-step conversion, time-to-convert distributions, breakdowns by any property, and — the part people underestimate — funnel trends over time, so you can see whether a change actually moved conversion or whether you're looking at noise. Flows gives you path analysis for "where did people go after this step."

Amplitude's standout is behavioral cohorts. You can define a cohort inline from any chart ("users who did upgrade_viewed twice in 7 days but never fired subscription_started") and then use that cohort everywhere else, including syncing it out to Braze, Customer.io, or a warehouse. Pathfinder handles journey analysis, and their retention charts handle the "Nth occurrence" and "return on" modes that power users ask for.

PostHog's funnels and retention are solid and have improved a lot, and you get correlation analysis — "users who converted were 4x more likely to have seen the integrations page" — which the other two don't do as directly. What PostHog doesn't match is the depth of cohort construction and the polish of Amplitude's journey tooling. For most small teams, that gap doesn't matter. For a growth team running weekly experiments, it does.

Session replay and qualitative analysis

PostHog treats session replay as a first-class product, not a bolt-on. You get console logs, network activity, a DOM explorer, and — the feature that actually changes how you work — the ability to filter replays by funnel step, so you can watch only the sessions where someone dropped off at checkout. The free tier includes 5,000 recordings per month.
