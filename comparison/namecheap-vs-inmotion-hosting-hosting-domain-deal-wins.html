Namecheap vs InMotion Hosting: Which Hosting or Domain Deal Wins in 2026?

Here's the uncomfortable truth about pitting Namecheap against InMotion Hosting: they aren't really the same kind of company, and most comparisons pretend they are.

Namecheap started as a domain registrar that got fed up with GoDaddy's upsells and built a hosting business on the side. InMotion Hosting started as a hosting company and has spent two decades doing very little else. One sells you a $11 domain and hopes you'll add a $30/year hosting plan. The other sells you a $100+ hosting relationship and hands you a domain as a thank-you.

That distinction determines almost every result below. So instead of a generic "both are great!" writeup, let's look at where each one actually earns your money — and where it doesn't.

Hosting prices

Namecheap's shared lineup is the Stellar family: Stellar, Stellar Plus, and Stellar Business. InMotion counters with Launch, Power, and Pro.

Plan tierNamecheap (promo / renewal)InMotion (promo / renewal)

Two things to notice.

First, promo pricing on both sides requires a long term — typically 12 months minimum, sometimes 24 or 36 — and both companies raise your rate at renewal. This is industry standard, not a scandal, but budget for the renewal number, not the promo number.

Second, the gap is smaller than the marketing suggests once you look at renewals. Namecheap Stellar Business renews around $9.50/month; InMotion Pro renews around $15.50. But InMotion Pro is also a genuinely better-resourced plan. It's not apples to apples.
