NordVPN vs ExpressVPN: Which VPN Is Worth Your Money in 2026?

NordVPN and ExpressVPN are the two names that come up in every VPN conversation, and for good reason. Both have been around for over a decade, both have passed independent no-logs audits, and both charge a premium over the dozens of $2/month providers you've never heard of.

So the real question isn't whether they're good. It's whether the extra money buys you anything meaningful — and if so, which one deserves it.

Here's the honest breakdown, including where each one falls short.

Short answer: NordVPN wins on value for most people — cheaper long-term plans, 10 devices instead of 8, and a bigger server network. ExpressVPN wins if streaming consistency and setup simplicity matter more to you than price. Now let's get into the details.

Server network: size vs. spread

NordVPN runs roughly 7,000 servers across 118 countries. ExpressVPN runs around 3,000 servers across 105 countries.

On paper, NordVPN has more than double the capacity. In practice, that gap matters less than you'd think. ExpressVPN's servers are more evenly distributed — it's one of the few providers with real coverage in places like Azerbaijan, Belarus, and a handful of African and Central Asian countries where NordVPN has no presence at all. If you need a specific obscure location, check ExpressVPN first.

Both use RAM-only servers, meaning nothing is written to disk and every server wipes itself on reboot. NordVPN calls this "diskless," ExpressVPN calls it TrustedServer. Same idea, same benefit: there's no storage for anyone to seize or subpoena.

Edge: NordVPN for raw capacity, ExpressVPN for geographic reach in unusual places.

Speed: NordLynx vs. Lightway

Both companies abandoned plain OpenVPN as their default years ago, and both built their own WireGuard-based protocols.

ExpressVPN's Lightway is a from-scratch protocol rather than a WireGuard wrapper, and its whole selling point is reconnection speed. It's noticeably faster to establish a tunnel — useful when you're switching networks constantly or waking a laptop from sleep. Throughput is comparable to NordLynx, sometimes slightly behind on long-haul routes.

Both protocols are open source and have been independently reviewed. Both also now ship post-quantum encryption options, which sounds like marketing but is a real hedge against future "harvest now, decrypt later" attacks on recorded traffic.

One practical detail for developers: if you run local services, split tunneling is what keeps localhost and your Docker network reachable while the VPN is on. NordVPN supports split tunneling on Windows, Android, Android TV, and macOS (with a more limited implementation). ExpressVPN supports it on Windows, macOS, Linux, Android, and routers — but not iOS, same as NordVPN. If you develop on Linux, ExpressVPN's split tunneling support is the more complete option.

Edge: Effectively a tie on throughput. ExpressVPN for connection speed and Linux split tunneling; NordVPN for long-haul consistency.

Streaming and unblocking

This used to be ExpressVPN's uncontested territory. It's tightened up considerably.

BBC iPlayer, Hulu, Disney+, and Amazon Prime all worked without server-hopping.

NordVPN got there too, but with more friction.Once connected, playback was fine — no buffering, no quality drops. It's a minor annoyance rather than a dealbreaker, but if you're the person your family calls when the TV stops working, ExpressVPN is the calmer choice.

Both handle torrenting, though NordVPN has dedicated P2P servers and a clearer policy around it. ExpressVPN allows P2P on all servers.

Edge: ExpressVPN, narrowly.

Privacy, audits, and who actually owns them

This is where the comparison gets more interesting, and where a lot of reviews gloss over things.

NordVPN is based in Panama, which has no mandatory data retention laws and isn't part of any intelligence-sharing alliance. Its no-logs policy has been audited by Deloitte (twice) and PwC, and its apps have been reviewed by Cure53.
