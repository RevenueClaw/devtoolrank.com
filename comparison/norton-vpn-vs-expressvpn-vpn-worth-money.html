Norton VPN vs ExpressVPN: Which VPN Is Worth Your Money in 2026?

These two get compared constantly, and honestly, they're not really the same kind of product. ExpressVPN is a dedicated VPN company that has spent a decade optimizing one thing. Norton Secure VPN is a piece of a much bigger security bundle — antivirus, a password manager, dark web monitoring, the works.

That difference matters more than any spec sheet. If you're shopping for the best VPN in 2026 and you've narrowed it down to these two, the question isn't "which has more servers." It's "which one fits how I actually use a VPN."

Let me walk through where each one wins, where each one falls short, and what the pricing really looks like after the intro discount expires.

The short version

ExpressVPN is the better VPN. Faster, more servers, more locations, better streaming, actual support for torrenting, and a privacy record backed by multiple independent audits. It's also more expensive and doesn't include anything else.

Norton Secure VPN is the better deal if you already want Norton's antivirus suite. Bundled into Norton 360 Deluxe, the VPN costs you almost nothing extra and covers up to 10 devices. On its own, though, it's a mediocre VPN at a price that stops being competitive after year one.

Now the details.

Privacy and logging: the gap is wider than the marketing suggests

Both companies claim a strict no-logs policy. That's table stakes. The difference is how much evidence backs the claim.

ExpressVPN is based in the British Virgin Islands, which has no data retention laws and no intelligence-sharing agreements with the US, UK, or EU. Its servers run entirely on RAM — the TrustedServer setup means nothing is written to disk, so a server seizure yields nothing. The company has published audits from Cure53, F-Secure, PwC, and KPMG, and the Lightway protocol it built in-house is open source, so anyone can read the code.

Norton Secure VPN is operated by Gen Digital, a US company headquartered in Arizona (with major operations in Prague). The US sits in the Five Eyes intelligence alliance. Norton states a no-logs policy and has undergone independent review, but it doesn't publish audits on the same cadence ExpressVPN does, and its core app isn't open source.

Does that Make Norton unsafe? No. For someone protecting their traffic on café Wi-Fi, it's fine. But if your threat model includes a government with subpoena power, jurisdiction and audit history matter, and ExpressVPN has the stronger story.

One more thing worth knowing: ExpressVPN's parent company, Kape Technologies, also owns CyberGhost, PIA, and ZenMate. Some people find that consolidation off-putting. It doesn't change the audit results, but you should know who's cashing the check.

Server network and coverage

ExpressVPN runs roughly 3,000 servers across 105 countries. Norton Secure VPN offers around 2,000 servers in 30+ countries.

That's not a trivial difference. Thirty countries covers the obvious destinations — US, UK, Canada, Germany, Japan, Australia — but it thins out fast in South America, Africa, and Southeast Asia. If you travel to Brazil or Vietnam and want a local IP, ExpressVPN has you covered and Norton probably doesn't.

Server count isn't everything. What matters more is whether the servers you connect to are fast and unblocked. ExpressVPN's network is smaller than some competitors (NordVPN runs 7,000+), but it's consistently well maintained. Norton's network is functional; it just isn't the reason anyone buys it.

Speed and protocols

ExpressVPN's Lightway protocol is the real differentiator here. It's built on wolfSSL, uses less code than OpenVPN, and reconnects almost instantly when you switch networks or wake a laptop from sleep. In published speed tests, ExpressVPN routinely retains 85–95% of baseline throughput on nearby servers — it's usually in the top three of any VPN roundup, and often first.

Norton Secure VPN added WireGuard support across Windows, Mac, iOS, and Android, and that helped a lot. WireGuard is lean and fast, and Norton's implementation is competent. Realistically, expect to keep 60–75% of your raw speed on a good server. That's usable for browsing and HD streaming, less pleasant for large downloads or 4K.

Latency is where ExpressVPN pulls further ahead. If you're on video calls all day or doing anything interactive, the difference in responsiveness is noticeable.

Streaming and torrenting

This is where the two products stop being comparable.

ExpressVPN unblocks Netflix, BBC iPlayer, Disney+, Hulu, Amazon Prime Video, and most regional libraries reliably. It maintains a dedicated MediaStreamer DNS service for devices that can't run a VPN app — smart TVs, older consoles, some routers. If streaming is why you're buying a VPN, ExpressVPN is the safer pick.

Norton Secure VPN's streaming support is inconsistent. It works sometimes, on some servers, on some platforms. Netflix in particular tends to detect and block it. Norton's own documentation is careful not to promise access to specific services, which tells you what you need to know.

Torrenting is similar. ExpressVPN supports P2P on every server and doesn't throttle it. Norton Secure VPN doesn't officially support torrenting at all — some servers will let it through, but you're on your own and it's not a documented feature. If file sharing is part of your workflow, this alone settles the comparison.
