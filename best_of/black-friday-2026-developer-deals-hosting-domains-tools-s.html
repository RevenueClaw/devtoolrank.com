Black Friday 2026 Developer Deals: Hosting, Domains, and Tools — What's Actually Discounted

Black Friday is November 27 this year, Cyber Monday follows on the 30th, and by the time you read this the promo pages are already live. Most of them are noise. A handful are genuinely worth setting a calendar reminder for.

I've been tracking these sales for a few years now, and the pattern is boringly consistent: cloud providers hand out credits to acquire new accounts, registrars slash first-year domain prices to near-zero, and SaaS companies discount annual plans they'd rather you commit to. Almost nothing else moves. If you're waiting for GitHub to knock 40% off Copilot, you'll be waiting a long time.

So here's the practical version. What's likely to be discounted in 2026, what the real numbers look like based on the last three cycles, and which "deals" are actually traps with a countdown timer attached.

One caveat up front: exact 2026 offers weren't all published at the time of writing. Where I give numbers, they're the historical ranges these companies have run, and I've flagged anything that tends to shift year to year. Verify before you enter a card.

The short version

  • DigitalOcean is the single best deal for most solo devs — expect a $200 credit for new accounts, valid 60 days.
  • Namecheap still owns the domain category, with .com registrations in the $5–6 range and .io/.co deals that beat anything else in November.
  • Cloudflare Registrar doesn't do Black Friday and doesn't need to — it sells at wholesale cost year-round.
  • Hetzner rarely discounts. Their list prices are already the discount.
  • Managed WordPress hosts (Cloudways, Kinsta, WP Engine) offer the biggest headline percentages and the most confusing terms. Read those carefully.
  • JetBrains runs a predictable sale on personal licenses. It's the one dev-tool discount I've never regretted.

How to tell a real deal from a fake one

Three questions, every time.

What's the renewal price? A $1.99/month hosting plan that renews at $14.99 is a $156/year commitment dressed up as a bargain. Hostinger, Bluehost, and most shared hosts play this game every November. It's not a scam exactly, but it's not a deal either.

Is it a credit or a discount? A $200 DigitalOcean credit and 40% off a DigitalOcean plan are very different things. Credits expire, usually in 60 days, and they only apply to new accounts. If you don't have a project ready to deploy, a credit is a deadline, not a gift.

What's the actual baseline? Some "50% off" prices are 50% off a list price nobody has paid since 2019. Check the current non-sale price on a site like CamelCamelCamel for consumer stuff, or just look at the pricing page without a promo code. Software pricing pages are usually honest about list price; the sale banner is where things get creative.

Now, the categories.

Hosting and cloud credits

DigitalOcean

The DigitalOcean credit is the most reliably useful promo in the developer world. For the last several cycles it's been $200 in credits for new accounts, valid for 60 days, applied automatically at signup or via a promo code during the sale window.

Sixty days is real time. That's two months of a $24/month droplet, or a Kubernetes cluster you can actually stress-test, or enough App Platform usage to migrate a side project off Heroku and see whether you like it. If you've been meaning to try something new, this is the cheapest way to do it.

The catch: it's new accounts only, and once the credit burns out you're on standard pricing. Droplets start at $4/month for the smallest instance, $6 for 1GB RAM. That's not a hardship. Just don't build a production architecture you can't afford in January.

Hetzner

Hetzner has run Black Friday promos exactly twice in the years I've been watching, and both times it was a modest account credit rather than a price cut. Their CX22 instance — 2 vCPU, 4GB RAM, 40GB SSD — sits around €3.79/month year-round. You cannot discount that meaningfully.

If you want Hetzner, buy Hetzner. Waiting for a sale is a waste of your November.

Vultr and Linode (Akamai)

Both run credit promos in the same shape as DigitalOcean's: $100–$300 for new accounts, 30–60 day windows. Vultr's high-frequency compute is genuinely faster than DigitalOcean's equivalent at the low end, and their $2.50/month IPv6-only instance is the cheapest real VPS you'll find from a major provider.

Linode, now Akamai Cloud, has been quieter since the acquisition. Expect $100 in credit over 60 days. Fine, not exciting.

Cloudways, Kinsta, WP Engine

This is where the big percentages live, and where you need to slow down.

Cloudways typically runs 40% off for the first four months, or 30% off for three months on annual billing. On a $14/month DigitalOcean-backed 1GB server, four months at 40% off saves you about $22. Not nothing, but the marketing copy says "40% OFF" in 72-point type, which sets an expectation the math doesn't meet.
