Migrating from Bluehost to Namecheap in 2026: The Step-by-Step Switch Guide

Most people don't leave Bluehost because the hosting is broken. They leave because of an invoice.

Before the walkthrough, read our full Bluehost vs Namecheap comparison for the head-to-head that motivates this switch.

You signed up at $2.95 a month on a 36-month term, the site ran fine, and then the renewal notice shows up at $10.99 a month — or $21.99 if you're on the higher tier. That's the entire story for probably 80% of the migrations I've helped with. The hosting did its job. The price didn't.

Namecheap is where a lot of those people land, and for good reason: the renewal price is roughly half of Bluehost's, the panel is plain cPanel instead of a custom skin, and domain pricing is genuinely among the cheapest you'll find. But moving a live site between hosts is the part people put off for six months because they're afraid of breaking something.

This guide is that six months, compressed. Below is what both hosts actually cost in 2026, the two-year math nobody shows you at checkout, and a migration sequence I've run on client sites without meaningful downtime.

Bluehost vs Namecheap in 2026: what you're actually comparing

Both are shared hosting on Linux, both give you a free domain for the first year, both include SSL. The differences show up in the details.

Bluehost leans hard on the WordPress relationship — one-click installs, a custom dashboard, phone support at any hour, and a checkout flow that will try to sell you four add-ons before you see a receipt. The entry tier is one website. Support is genuinely reachable by phone, which matters if you're not comfortable in a terminal.

Namecheap is a domain registrar that also sells hosting, and that shapes everything. Free WHOIS privacy for life on domains, .com registrations that undercut most competitors, and hosting that runs on standard cPanel you can hand to any freelancer without a tutorial. The tradeoff: no phone support, only live chat and tickets.

One thing worth flagging before we go further: Namecheap's entry tier is a single website, and the higher tier raises both the site count and the storage. Check the current spec sheet when you order, because Namecheap adjusts those limits more often than it changes prices.

The 2-Year Renewal Math (2026 Prices)

Bluehost: 36 months at $2.95 intro, then renewal at $10.99/month — the standard 2-year snapshot (12 intro + 12 renewal) = 12 x $2.95 + 12 x $10.99 = $167.28. Namecheap (1-year terms): 12 x $2.88 + 12 x $8.88 = $141.12. Namecheap stays cheaper across 24 months AND renews closer to its intro rate.

Note: this is a hosting-style annual-renewal market — promo entry prices are real, but the renewal rate is the number that matters over 24 months.

Verdict

Namecheap wins the 2-year math ($141 vs $167) and the renewal curve; Bluehost still edges on onboarding polish.

Check current Bluehost and Namecheap intro-versus-renewal pricing before your term ends — renewals are where this decision actually gets made.

Step-by-Step Migration

1) Back up files, databases, and email. 2) Buy the Namecheap plan. 3) Migrate files (FTP/rsync) and databases (phpMyAdmin). 4) Recreate email accounts. 5) Lower DNS TTL, then switch nameservers. 6) Keep Bluehost alive one overlap month; cancel before renewal.

FAQ
