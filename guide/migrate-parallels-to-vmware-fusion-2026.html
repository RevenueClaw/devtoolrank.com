Migrating from Parallels to VMware Fusion in 2026: The Step-by-Step Switch Guide

If you've been paying for Parallels Desktop since the Apple Silicon transition, you already know the math has changed. What hasn't changed is the renewal notice: $99.99 a year for Standard Edition, every year, at the same rate — no intro discount that quietly resets, just the same number showing up on your statement.

Before the walkthrough, read our full Parallels vs VMware Fusion comparison for the head-to-head that motivates this switch.

Meanwhile VMware Fusion Pro — the same Fusion Pro that used to run $199 — is now free for personal use under Broadcom. That's not a marketing angle. It's the actual price on the download page.

This guide is for anyone running Parallels Desktop on a Mac who wants to move their existing VMs to Fusion without rebuilding from scratch. I'll cover what you actually gain and lose in the switch, the real 2026 prices side by side, the 24-month math, and a migration sequence that works whether you're on an M-series Mac or an Intel one. There are a couple of steps where people get burned (Windows activation, Parallels Tools, static IPs), and I'll flag each of those before you hit them.

Why people are switching in 2026

Three things pushed this migration from "someday" to "this weekend" for a lot of developers:

The price gap is now infinite. Parallels Standard is $99.99/year. Fusion Pro is $0 for personal use. Even Parallels' perpetual license — $129.99 one-time — only gets you the current major version; Parallels ships a new major release most years, and upgrades cost money again.

Fusion still does the work. Free doesn't mean stripped. Fusion Pro on Apple Silicon runs ARM Linux guests (Ubuntu, Fedora, Debian, Kali) at essentially native speed, supports snapshots, ships a vmrun CLI, and includes a virtual network editor for building custom vmnet segments. If your day-to-day is "spin up an Ubuntu box, break it, restore a snapshot," Fusion covers that completely.

Broadcom's stewardship is the wildcard. Fusion releases have slowed since the acquisition, and support quality is a real question mark. That's the honest tradeoff: you're trading money for release cadence and polish. Most solo developers and small teams take that trade. Some don't.

Step-by-Step Migration

1) Back up your Parallels VM and note its resources. 2) Download Fusion Pro (free personal license from Broadcom). 3) Import or recreate the VM; re-run Windows activation if needed. 4) Reconnect shared folders, USB, and GPU settings. 5) Keep Parallels until the Fusion VM has run a full workweek. 6) Cancel Parallels before its renewal date.

FAQ

Q: Is VMware Fusion really free in 2026?

A: Yes — Fusion Pro is free for personal use; commercial use requires a paid Broadcom license.

Q: Does Parallels renew at the same price?

A: Yes, subscriptions renew at the same rate ($99.99/yr Standard, $119.99/yr Pro).

The 2-Year Renewal Math (2026 Prices)

Parallels Standard: 12 months at $99.99 + 12 months at $99.99 (renewal) = $199.98 over 24 months. Parallels Pro: $119.99 x 2 = $239.98. VMware Fusion Pro (personal): $0 + $0 = $0 — a free product has no renewal trap. If you need Fusion for commercial work, budget the Broadcom commercial license into year two instead of assuming $0.

Note: this is a hosting-style annual-renewal market — promo entry prices are real, but the renewal rate is the number that matters over 24 months.

Verdict

Fusion wins on price for personal use ($0 vs $199.98 over two years); Parallels still wins on polish and USB/GPU ergonomics — switch if $0 matters more than ergonomics.

Check current Parallels and VMware Fusion pricing before you move — subscription and perpetual options shift, and Fusion's free tier changes commercial rules fast.
