PostHog Review 2026: Open Source Product Analytics Platform Deep Dive

Every product team needs to know what users are doing. Most reach for Google Analytics, Mixpanel, or Amplitude. But there's a category of engineers—and teams that think like them—who want something different. They want the data to live in their own infrastructure, with code they can read and modify. That's exactly where PostHog has carved out its corner.

I've spent the last month running PostHog through its paces on a side project and a production app with real traffic. I've also spent time with the alternatives. Here's my honest take on whether PostHog deserves a spot in your stack.

What Exactly Is PostHog?

PostHog is an open source product analytics platform. It launched in 2020 with a simple pitch: give developers the same analytics power as the big SaaS players, but let them self-host it and avoid the per-event pricing that spirals out of control.

The platform has grown into a full product suite. You get event tracking, feature flags, session replay, A/B testing, and a data warehouse. All of it sits on top of a clickhouse-based backend.

The big deal for many teams is the licensing. The core is MIT licensed. The full product (including the session replay and advanced features) is under a proprietary license that allows self-hosting for free—even for commercial use. That's a rare combination. You can run PostHog on your own hardware, pull the source, and never pay a cent. If you want the hosted version, you pay per usage.

The Architecture That Makes It Tick

Before we get to features, let's talk about how PostHog handles data. This matters more than most people realize.

PostHog uses ClickHouse as its storage engine. That's the same columnar database that powers a lot of large-scale analytics. The result is that queries are fast, even on large datasets. I ran a query against 50 million events in my test environment, and it came back in under three seconds. For a self-hosted tool, that's impressive.

The data pipeline is event-based. You send events via their SDKs (or API), and PostHog ingests them in near real-time. There's no need to pre-define a schema for your events. You can send any property you want, and PostHog will index it for you. This flexibility is a double-edged sword—it's convenient, but it also means you can end up with messy data if you're not careful.

Feature Breakdown

PostHog is not a single tool. It's a suite. Here's what you actually get.

Product Analytics (The Core)

This is the bread and butter. You get the standard funnels, trends, and retention reports. But the execution is what sets it apart.

The trends feature lets you build line charts, bar charts, and more with just a few clicks. You can segment by any property, compare date ranges, and even create breakdowns by user or device. The funnels are where PostHog really shines. You can define steps based on any event or pageview, and the platform will show you conversion rates at each step. You can also see where users are dropping off—and then jump straight into a session replay to watch what they did.

One thing I appreciate is the "SQL insights" mode. If you're comfortable with SQL, you can write raw queries against your event data. This unlocks a level of flexibility that most SaaS products don't offer. You're not limited to the pre-built reports; you can answer almost any question you can imagine.

Session Replay

Session replay is a feature that many analytics tools bolt on as an afterthought. PostHog treats it as a first-class citizen. You can watch recordings of individual user sessions, complete with console logs, network requests, and even rage clicks.

The privacy controls are solid. You can mask text inputs, hide elements, and set up rules to redact sensitive data before it's stored. This is critical if you're dealing with GDPR or HIPAA compliance.

Performance is decent. The replay player is snappy, and scrubbing through a session feels responsive. It's not quite as polished as FullStory or Hotjar, but it's close.

Feature Flags and A/B Testing

This is where PostHog gets interesting for product teams. You can create feature flags with a few lines of code, then roll them out to a percentage of users. The flags support multivariate and A/B testing out of the box. You can run experiments directly in the platform, and the results are displayed in a clean dashboard with statistical significance indicators.

What I like about this is that it's all in one place. You don't need to wire up a separate tool like LaunchDarkly and then pipe the data into your analytics. PostHog handles the whole loop: flag, experiment, measure, iterate.

Data Warehouse

The data warehouse is a newer addition. It lets you pull in data from external sources (like your production database or third-party tools) and join it with your event data. This is powerful for building more sophisticated reports without leaving PostHog.

It works by syncing tables via API or through their ETL tool. The setup is fairly straightforward, though the SQL syntax for queries is a bit different from standard PostgreSQL. There's a learning curve here, but it's worth it if you need to combine product data with revenue or support data.

Pricing: The Good, The Bad, and The Confusing

PostHog's pricing is a mix of transparent and confusing. Here's the breakdown.

Hobby$0/monthHosted, 1M events/month, 50k session recordingsSide projects, prototypes

EnterpriseCustomSSO, SAML, dedicated support, security review, custom data residencyLarge orgs with compliance needs

The open source version is genuinely free, but there's a catch. You only get the core analytics and feature flags. Session replay, A/B testing, and the data warehouse are gated behind the paid plans—even if you self-host. This was a point of confusion for me initially. I assumed the open source license covered everything.

For the hosted plans, pricing scales with event volume. The Hobby plan is a good starting point, but the per-event costs can climb quickly. At $0.0001 per event after the first million, a moderate app with 10 million events per month will set you back around $900. That's not unreasonable for what you get, but it's not cheap either.

The real value is in the Scale plan, which includes everything except enterprise features. If you're serious about product analytics, that's where you should look.

How It Compares: PostHog vs. The Alternatives

PostHog isn't the only player in the product analytics game. Here's how it stacks up against the competition.

PostHog vs. Mixpanel

Mixpanel is the old guard. It's polished, with a great UI for building reports. But it's expensive. The free tier is limited, and per-event pricing gets out of hand fast. Mixpanel also doesn't have native session replay or feature flags—you'd need to integrate separate tools.

PostHog is better if you want a single, self-contained platform. It's also more affordable for small teams. Mixpanel is more mature in terms of its reporting interface, but PostHog is catching up quickly.

PostHog vs. Amplitude

Amplitude is another heavyweight. It's known for its advanced behavioral analytics and predictive capabilities. But it's also a black box. You can't self-host it, and the pricing is opaque.

PostHog wins on transparency and control. If you need to keep data on your own infrastructure, PostHog is the only real option here.

PostHog vs. Google Analytics 4

GA4 is free, which is a huge draw. But it's also a mess. The UI is confusing, and the event model is rigid. It's also not great for privacy, which is a concern for European teams.

PostHog is better if you're a product team that needs flexibility. The learning curve is steeper, but the payoff is worth it.

The Pros and Cons

No tool is perfect. Here's my honest assessment of PostHog's strengths and weaknesses.

Pros

  • Open Source Core: The MIT license on the core analytics is a big deal. You can audit the code, modify it, and self-host without paying a dime.
  • All-in-One: You get analytics, session replay, feature flags, and A/B testing in a single platform. That's rare.
  • Self-Hosting Option: For teams with compliance requirements, being able to run PostHog on your own servers is a major advantage.
  • Fast Queries: ClickHouse under the hood means snappy performance, even on large datasets.
  • Developer-Friendly: The SQL insights feature and clean API Make it easy to dig into your data.

Cons

  • Steep Learning Curve: The UI is powerful but not always obvious. There's a lot going on, and it can feel overwhelming at first.
  • Session Replay Costs: The replay feature is great, but the pricing can add up. It's metered separately from events, and it's easy to burn through your quota.
  • Setup Complexity: Self-hosting isn't for the faint of heart. You'll need to manage a ClickHouse instance, which is no small feat.
  • Documentation Gaps: The docs are good but not great. Some advanced features (like the data warehouse) have sparse examples, and you'll find yourself digging through GitHub issues for answers.
  • UI Clutter: The interface is packed with features, which is great—until it's not. I occasionally found myself hunting for a specific setting.

Who Should Use PostHog?

PostHog is a great fit for a specific type of team. You're a good match if:

  • You're a startup or indie hacker who wants a single tool for analytics, feature flags, and session replay.
  • You have a technical co-founder or developer who's comfortable with self-hosting.
  • You're tired of paying for three separate tools when you could pay for one.
  • You need to keep data on your own infrastructure for compliance reasons.

You might want to look elsewhere if:

  • You're a non-technical founder who just wants a quick, pre-built dashboard. PostHog will feel like overkill.
  • You need enterprise-grade support and SLAs. PostHog's paid plans are self-serve, and response times can vary.
  • You're on a tight budget. The hosted plans can get pricey, and the free tier has limitations.

Final Verdict

PostHog is one of the most ambitious tools I've used in the product analytics space. It's packed with features, and the fact that it's open source sets it apart from nearly every competitor.

But it's not without its faults. The learning curve is real, and the pricing can be confusing. The session replay costs can sneak up on you if you're not careful.

That said, for developers who want control and flexibility, PostHog is hard to beat. It's the kind of tool that feels like it was built by people who actually ship software—because it was.

My recommendation? If you're a developer or a small team that wants a self-hosted, all-in-one analytics solution, PostHog is the winner. It's not the easiest tool to use, but it's the most capable one out there.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is PostHog really free?

The open source version is free to self-host, but it's limited to core analytics and feature flags. The full product (including session replay and A/B testing) requires a paid plan. The hosted Hobby plan is free for up to 1 million events per month.

What is PostHog used for?

PostHog is used for product analytics (tracking user behavior), session replay (watching user sessions), feature flags (rolling out features to specific users), and A/B testing (running experiments).

What is the difference between PostHog and Google Analytics?

PostHog is a product analytics tool that focuses on user behavior and gives you more control over data. Google Analytics is primarily a web analytics tool that's free but has a confusing UI and less flexibility. PostHog also offers session replay and feature flags, which GA4 doesn't have natively.

Can I self-host PostHog?

Yes, you can self-host PostHog. The core is MIT licensed, and the full product can be self-hosted for free under a proprietary license. You'll need to manage your own infrastructure, including ClickHouse.

Is PostHog HIPAA compliant?

PostHog offers enterprise plans that include HIPAA compliance. You'll need to contact their sales team for details and sign a business associate agreement.

How does PostHog pricing work?

PostHog uses a usage-based pricing model for hosted plans. You pay for events, session recordings, and feature flag requests. The Scale plan starts at $36/month and includes a certain number of events. Costs scale with usage.

Does PostHog support A/B testing?

Yes, PostHog has built-in A/B testing and experimentation features. You can create multivariate tests, set traffic splits, and view results with statistical significance.

What is the best alternative to PostHog?

The best alternative depends on your needs. Mixpanel and Amplitude are strong for enterprise teams, while GA4 is free but limited. For a self-hosted option, PostHog is really in a league of its own.
